# Publication-safe V4 results summary

The V4 analysis compares additional frequency information with an additional particle-size fraction. Results are metal- and endpoint-dependent.

- **Mn:** two-fraction LF measurement produced higher pooled out-of-block R² (0.405) than the frequency-aware coarse configuration (0.300), but at an 11-sample chemistry budget the coarse frequency-aware configuration recovered 6/11 high-Mn samples versus 5/11 for the two-fraction LF configuration.
- **Cr:** coarse frequency-aware screening had lower block-mean prediction error than the two-fraction LF configuration (approximately 0.095 versus 0.111 log10 units), and the direction survived complete block-deletion refits.
- **Cu:** the two-fraction LF configuration achieved pooled R² of 0.488, while direct fine-fraction ordering could match the same 8/11 low-budget recovery in the descriptive ranking comparison.
- **Resource accounting:** for the retrospective 80% Mn recovery target, the two-fraction LF strategy required 20 selected samples and the frequency-aware coarse strategy 21. Under equal development costs, this leaves an assay-count margin of one assay for the extra per-candidate screening cost.

These findings do not establish a universal best magnetic configuration. They show why predictive accuracy, sample-recovery performance, and resource use must be evaluated separately.
